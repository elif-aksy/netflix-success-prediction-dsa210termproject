Project Overview

What drives Netflix content ratings?

Two Kaggle datasets were merged — Netflix catalog metadata and IMDB scores — to create a 3,955-title dataset. The analysis spans exploratory data analysis, statistical hypothesis testing, and four machine learning models evaluated on both regression and classification tasks.

Hypothesis Testing

Statistical findings (α = 0.05)

#HypothesisTestResultKey Statistic
H1 Movies and TV shows have the same mean IMDB score Two-sample t-test Rejected p ≈ 0 — TV shows score 0.80 pts higher
H2 All genres share the same mean IMDB score One-way ANOVA + Tukey HSD Rejected p ≈ 0 — 296/561 genre pairs differ
H3 Release year and IMDB score are uncorrelated Pearson + Spearman Not Rejected Spearman r = −0.018, p = 0.255
H4 Vote count and IMDB score are uncorrelated Pearson + Spearman Rejected Spearman r = 0.239, p ≈ 0

Machine Learning

Predicting content success

Binary classification: success = IMDB score ≥ 7.0 (~39.5% of titles). F1 is the primary metric due to mild class imbalance.

Key Findings

What we learned

1
TV shows outperform movies TV shows score 0.80 pts higher on average (7.05 vs 6.25, p ≈ 0). Audiences self-select into series they expect to enjoy.
2
Genre is a strong quality signal Korean TV Shows consistently lead; Horror Movies score the lowest. 296 of 561 genre pairs differ significantly (Tukey HSD).
3
Release year has no meaningful effect Spearman r = −0.018 (p = 0.255). Content age does not predict quality.
4
Country of origin matters South Korea (7.24) and Japan (7.09) lead. The US contributes the most titles (n=1,626) but sits below average (6.57).
5
Popularity correlates with quality — but weakly Spearman r = 0.239 between vote count and IMDB score. Popularity alone is not a reliable predictor.
6
Director & cast reputation are the strongest predictors LOO-encoded director and cast features boosted XGBoost F1 by +0.112 (0.732 → 0.844). Who made it matters more than genre or country.
7
Metadata explains ~39% of score variance Best regression model: Random Forest, R² = 0.386. The rest is subjective viewer experience — unobservable from catalog data.
